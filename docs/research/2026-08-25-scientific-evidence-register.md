# DUOLAC / Cell Biotech Scientific Evidence Register

Research date: 2026-08-25
Audience: scientist, product manager, regulatory reviewer, and LLM generating internal drafts.

## Reading rule

Probiotic evidence is strain-, formulation-, dose-, population-, and outcome-specific. A study of a Cell Biotech strain or of Duolac Care does not automatically substantiate every DUOLAC product, every consumer, or a Thai advertising claim.

## Detailed evidence notes

### 1. Dual-coated probiotics in diarrhea-predominant IBS

Citation: Han K, Wang J, Seo JG, et al. Efficacy of double-coated probiotics for irritable bowel syndrome: a randomized double-blind controlled trial. Journal of Gastroenterology. 2017;52:432–443. doi:10.1007/s00535-016-1224-y.

Design: 50 adults with IBS-D; 2 capsules/day, each containing 5 billion bacteria; 4 weeks; dual-coating vs non-coating probiotic supplement.

Interpretation: This is useful evidence that coating can change performance relative to an uncoated comparator in a specific formulation and a specific IBS-D study. It is not enough to state that any DUOLAC product treats IBS or that all consumers will see a result.

Conflict / funding disclosure: The paper states Cell Biotech financial support; one co-author was a Cell Biotech employee, and another author had received funds. This does not invalidate the study, but it matters for evidence-weighting and public communication.

### 2. Dual-coating intellectual property

Record: US20200325439A1 / US11920124B2, “Method of producing lactic acid bacteria dual-coated with protein and polysaccharide by using protein hydrolysate.”

Interpretation: This independently corroborates that a Cell Biotech dual-coating production method was granted in the United States. It is appropriate to say “patented Dual Coating technology” only after confirming the relevant patent scope and trademark/wording that the Thai importer may use.

### 3. Company-reported 221× survival claim

Cell Biotech’s 2025 release describes a 40-person randomized, double-blind study jointly conducted with Sahmyook Medical Center. It reports average intestinal survival improvement around 100× and a maximum 221× vs uncoated probiotics, plus laboratory survival metrics.

Decision: Keep as pending_verification. Before any Thai creative uses “221×”, obtain:
- the complete clinical report or peer-reviewed publication;
- the exact product/strains and dosing;
- comparator definition;
- statistical analysis;
- Thai regulatory/legal confirmation of permitted wording;
- written parent-brand permission to localize the claim.

### 4. PP-P8 is not a DUOLAC consumer claim

Cell Biotech’s PP-P8 program uses patented strains CBT-LR5 and CBT-SL4 in a therapeutic development program and lists a Phase I trial in metastatic colorectal/rectal cancer. This is scientifically interesting as an R&D-capability signal but must never be used to imply that DUOLAC dietary supplements prevent or treat cancer.

## Scientific content rules for LLM outputs

- Link every scientific statement to the specific evidence record above.
- Say “studied” or “company-reported” when that is the evidence level; never upgrade to “proven.”
- Avoid disease names in marketing output unless an approved Thai claim explicitly permits them.
- Keep clinical and laboratory outcomes separate.
- “Survival to the gut” is a product-technology topic, not direct proof of a downstream health outcome.
